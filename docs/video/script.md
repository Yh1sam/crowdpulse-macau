## 1. Concept, and why it should score well

**Show it working, then prove it honestly.**

**Opening.** A 3-second sourced fact on navy (Golden Week 2025: crowd control on Rua de S. Paulo lifted, then reimposed 45 minutes later; Macao Daily News), then a 10-second illustrative moment, clearly labelled as fiction: Ah Po, a wheelchair user, heads to see the Ruins of St. Paul's and can only see people's backs. The real software then runs the whole loop in 26 seconds:
- the iOS app, in the iPhone Simulator, counts people on a public-domain clip;
- only numbers leave it;
- Senado Square's forecast turns CRITICAL, with a confidence score and reasons;
- the system suggests a stair-free detour with notices in Chinese, English and Portuguese;
- the recommendation waits for a person's approval (the APPROVE click is scripted for this recording, and a small footer says so), and the audit log records it under the name typed in "Deciding as" (a fictional name in this recording).

**Middle.** A one-word STEMB roll call introduces the team. The video then:
- states the problem with sourced facts (brief police crowd control at the Ruins on three afternoons of Golden Week 2026) and a cited medical mechanism;
- credits the police crowd forecast and early-warning system already running from the Ruins to Senado Square (officially, real-time monitoring with alerts), and its planned expansion, and claims only what CrowdPulse proposes (movable phones that send only numbers, 5-15 minute forecasts with a confidence score, a person's approval);
- proves privacy live (202, then 422, then 403);
- explains the confidence gate.

Nearly half the runtime (47%) is evidence. Every number wears a status badge (SIMULATED, REAL DATA, MODEL CHECK, PENDING, CORRECTED) and a source footer, and every limit is spoken:
- forecasting is synthetic (on screen, accuracy is set against a forecast that never warns: always SAFE is already 84.6% accurate, so the video ranks by F1);
- the detector under-counts, and calibration is one camera, tested post hoc: "a lead, not proof" (on screen, a fixed guess with no detector scores 4.8 people per frame against the calibrated 3.3);
- in the simulation, approval left Senado only modestly less crowded by the end, and it stayed critical (on screen: 32 vs 31 minutes above CRITICAL, final pair of runs); the effect rests on a diversion we assumed, not measured (on screen: routes into Senado made 30% less attractive for 20 minutes), and there was no live early warning, named as "our failure case";
- 24 of 40 simulated wheelchair users had no step-free route;
- the real-data test is announced before its result, with its rules committed before the test days.

**Who answers.** A 10-second card (S17b, "If it fails, who answers?") names who answers (the approving operator, named in the audit log, and the department running it), who may be more vulnerable, and how the public is told (every notice says an AI forecast is involved; a sign at each phone is planned).

**Close.** Back to Ah Po, with Senado still red, then a relay tagline from the team and an end card with the repository address.

**How it maps to the judging criteria:**

| criterion | how the video earns it |
|---|---|
| Feasibility | Visible before any claim is made. A named intended user (the Public Security Police Force), the authorities a pilot would need, and a one-year cost only once the team approves its estimate (docs/cost-estimate.md). |
| Innovation | Framed as an addition to a credited existing system (movable, numbers-only phones; confidence and a person's approval), never as a horizon the police lack. |
| Scientific rigour | Baselines ("no change"; accuracy set against always-SAFE), a positive control, a pre-registered real-data test (rules committed before its test days), a public correction and a named failure case. |
| Responsible AI (the challenge brief) | Concrete: privacy enforced in code, a confidence gate, approval by a person named in the audit log, accessibility and three languages, every public notice saying an AI forecast is involved, and a card on who answers if it fails and who may be more vulnerable (S17b). Strangers' faces are pixelated on screen too. |
| Interdisciplinarity / best STEMB integration | Each speaker's discipline is lit on screen, and an accessibility thread links Medicine and Engineering. |
| Social impact (Macau) | Specific to Macau: its places, its languages, its reduced-mobility visitors. |
| Teamwork | Shown through balanced speaking time (93-109 written words per member; 93-115 counting [MEMBER 2]'s 15-word Melbourne slot), an on-camera correction by two members (both on camera for both lines, S12) and a relay close. |

### F. One-sentence answers to prepare (Q&A)

- **Doesn't the police system already do this?** Partly, and S07 says so. The police "crowd forecast and early-warning system" covers Border Gate and the Ruins to Senado Square. Official replies describe what it does today as real-time density monitoring with alerts, from devices on CCTV posts and back-end analysis of about 1,700 Sky Eye cameras; zone-level forecasting is planned, and no horizon or accuracy is published. A district consultative-committee member publicly proposed 15-30 minute warnings at the Ruins, Senado Square and 板樟堂街 in 2026, so minutes-ahead warning is wanted, not new. CrowdPulse adds movable phones that staff can place anywhere, such as temporary pedestrian zones, that count on the phone and send only numbers, plus 5-15 minute forecasts with a confidence score, a person's approval, and stair-free trilingual detours. The police say they are open to sharing crowd data across departments, so our counts could feed their system (our inference, not an agreement). Never say the police lack coverage at any particular street.
- **Who is responsible if it gets it wrong?** The AI only recommends: an operator approves or rejects every action, and the audit log records the name they typed in "Deciding as" (self-declared; the prototype has no login). In law, the police hold the duty for pedestrian movement (Law 14/2018), and whichever department runs CrowdPulse would be the data controller under Law 8/2005; the final split is for the authorities to settle.
- **Is filming the street legal?** Images of identifiable people are personal data under Law 8/2005 (Art. 4(1)(1)), so counting on the phone is probably still "processing" even though nothing is stored. Before any pilot we would ask the Personal Data Protection Bureau, notify it as the law requires, and post trilingual signs modelled on Law 2/2012, Art. 25 (draft in brief-evidence.md, section 6). This is not a compliance claim.
- **Which departments would be involved?** The intended user is the Public Security Police Force, which runs crowd control (Law 14/2018, Art. 3). During holidays the Unitary Police Service runs a joint command centre.
