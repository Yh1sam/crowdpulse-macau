## 1. Concept, and why it should score well

**Why, then how, then what: say why it matters, show how ours is different, then show it working and prove it honestly.**

**Hook.** No title card (since 19 Oct): the video opens straight on a 12-second illustrative moment under an ILLUSTRATION badge. Mrs Chan, a wheelchair user, heads to see the Ruins of St. Paul's and can only see people's backs; S06's first words say she is made up. After the voice ends, a short text overlay states the real 2025 event (Golden Week 2025: control lifted, then reimposed 45 minutes later; Macao Daily News): the sourced fact that opened the cut as a 3 s silent title until 19 Oct, back on screen since the second review (section 4).

**Why (S06, S05, S07).** The video:
- says that Mrs Chan is made up and the crowds are not (the video's statement that the story is fictional), then states the problem with sourced facts (brief police crowd control at the Ruins on three afternoons of Golden Week 2026) and a cited medical mechanism;
- introduces the team: each member says in one sentence what they worked on, with their STEMB discipline on screen beside them, and the research question rises under the group shot and stays on the title card (≈5 s);
- credits the police crowd forecast and early-warning system already running from the Ruins to Senado Square (officially, real-time monitoring with alerts; its planned expansion stays on screen), then states the team's belief: crowd safety shouldn't cost anyone their privacy, or leave anyone behind.

**How (S07, S08, S09).** The video:
- proposes one loop and claims only that: movable phones that send only numbers, forecasts that say how sure they are, and detours checked for stairs that a person must approve. S07 ends on one two-column comparison, "Police system (public information)" beside "What we propose", with no ticks, crosses or ranking;
- proves privacy live (202, then 422, then 403);
- explains the confidence gate.

**What (S02-S17b).** "Here's what we built.": the real software runs the whole loop in 26 seconds (S02-S04, 1:19-1:45):
- the iOS app, in the iPhone Simulator, counts people on a public-domain clip;
- only numbers leave it;
- Senado Square's forecast turns CRITICAL, with a confidence score and reasons;
- the system suggests a stair-free detour with notices in Chinese, English and Portuguese;
- the recommendation waits for a person's approval (the APPROVE click is scripted for this recording, and a small footer says so), and the audit log records it under the name typed in "Deciding as" (a fictional name in this recording).

Then come the evidence (S10-S15) and the next step (S16): about 44% of the runtime. Every number wears a status badge (SIMULATED, REAL DATA, MODEL CHECK, PENDING), an outside fact gets a short source footer, and every limit is spoken. Since the 19 Oct declutter each evidence shot keeps fewer metrics: only the spoken number, its status and the hedges it needs. The secondary figures (the +5 / +10 min pairs, the 59% count ratio, the minutes above CRITICAL, the 30% setting) left the picture and are Q&A answers (section 4, 5F); S10's 91% vs 79% recall clause, cut from the voice at the review round, is spoken again since the second review (19 Oct):
- forecasting is synthetic (on screen, accuracy is set against a forecast that never warns: always SAFE is already 84.6% accurate, so the video ranks by F1);
- the detector under-counts, and calibration is one camera, tested post hoc: "a lead, not proof: each camera would need calibrating on site" (on screen, a fixed guess with no detector scores 4.8 people per frame against the calibrated 3.3);
- in the simulation, approval left Senado only modestly less crowded by the end, and it stayed critical (on screen: approved 0.75 vs no action 0.82 at 14:40); the effect rests on a diversion we assumed, not measured (on screen: "Assumed diversion, not measured"), and there was no live early warning, named as "our failure case", so staff on the ground stay in charge;
- 24 of 40 simulated wheelchair users had no step-free route;
- the real-data test is announced before its result, with its rules committed before the test days.

**Can it happen?** (S17) Phones on temporary mounts that can serve one festival, then move to the next; Macau's police as the intended user (the card names the Public Security Police Force; no partner yet); the whole chain running end to end in simulation, with 124 automated tests passing. No cost is on screen: the team lead decided on 19 Oct to keep the team's estimate (docs/cost-estimate.md) as a Q&A answer.

**Who answers.** A 12-second card (S17b, "If it fails, who answers?") names who answers (the approving operator, named in the audit log, and the department running it), who may be more vulnerable, and how the public is told (every notice says an AI forecast is involved; a sign at each phone is planned).

**Close.** Back to Mrs Chan, with Senado still red: the system flags the risk, and staff check her route and stay beside her (no approval stamp, and her route is never shown as cleared). Then a relay tagline from the team and an end card with the repository address.
