# Uncertainty and stronger baselines for the headline numbers

Generated by `experiments/uncertainty.py` from `metrics.json` in this folder (re-run: `.venv/bin/python -I experiments/uncertainty.py`). Forecast figures come from `ml/models/test_predictions.csv.gz`, the shipped models' predictions on the three test days of the **synthetic** simulation (2026-09-27 (Sunday), 2026-09-30 (Wednesday), 2026-10-04 (Sunday); 7 zones, forecasts issued 08:00-21:59 local time). Nothing was retrained. Intervals: block bootstrap, 10,000 resamples, seed 0, percentile 95% intervals. Primary block = one zone on one test day (21 blocks). With only 21 blocks a percentile bootstrap can under-cover (come out too narrow), so treat the 95% intervals as approximate. Zone-day blocks treat the zones of one day as independent of each other and cannot capture day-to-day variation (section 3). **The intervals describe resampling variation inside three simulated days only; they say nothing about how the simulator differs from a real street.** `metrics.json` stores unrounded values; every figure on this page is rounded once, for display.

In plain words: at +15 min the model's macro-F1 is 0.865 (95% interval 0.833 to 0.895) against 0.795 for "it will stay as it is now"; the gain of +0.070 has an interval of +0.049 to +0.089, so on these simulated days the model beats persistence in every resample. The gain grows with the horizon because "now" is a worse guess the further ahead you look.

| +15 min | +0.049 to +0.089 | +0.048 to +0.097 | +0.053 to +0.089 |

Meaning: every grouping of the minutes gives a gain interval above zero at every horizon, so the conclusion does not hinge on the block choice. Widest interval per horizon: +5 min: all zones in one local hour of one day; +10 min: all zones in one local hour of one day; +15 min: all zones in one local hour of one day.

## 4. Forecasts flagged as uncertain, and the live review flag

Rule in `ml/models/metrics.json` and the video script: a forecast is **flagged as uncertain by the model (confidence < 0.6)**, where confidence is the largest class probability. The accuracy of the rest gets only the zone-day block-bootstrap interval: a Wilson interval would treat the 16,920 unflagged +15 min forecasts, neighbouring minutes of which are strongly correlated, as independent and come out far too narrow.

| horizon | flagged | accuracy of flagged (Wilson 95%) | same, zone-day block bootstrap | accuracy of the rest (zone-day block bootstrap 95%) | flagged with a true CRITICAL target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| +5 min | 262 (1.5%) | 0.557 (0.497 to 0.616) | 0.495 to 0.605 | 0.976 (0.965 to 0.986) | 83 |
| +10 min | 287 (1.7%) | 0.571 (0.514 to 0.627) | 0.524 to 0.610 | 0.973 (0.961 to 0.984) | 97 |
| +15 min | 232 (1.4%) | 0.565 (0.500 to 0.627) | 0.487 to 0.656 | 0.969 (0.955 to 0.982) | 49 |

Meaning: at +15 min, 232 of 17,152 forecasts (1.4%) are flagged as uncertain by the model (confidence < 0.6), and those are right only 56.5% of the time (Wilson 50.0% to 62.7%; block bootstrap 48.7% to 65.6%) against 96.9% for the rest (block bootstrap 95.5% to 98.2%): the flag picks out the forecasts that deserve a second look.

Wilson treats every forecast as independent; the zone-day block bootstrap does not. For the flagged forecasts the bootstrap interval is wider than Wilson at +15 min (0.169 vs 0.127), and narrower at +5 min (0.109 vs 0.119) and +10 min (0.086 vs 0.114) (interval widths, bootstrap vs Wilson). The bootstrap is not reliably the more cautious of the two here: with only 21 blocks (flagged +15 min forecasts per block: 0 to 33, median 12, 8 blocks with none), a percentile bootstrap can itself under-cover. For the rest, a Wilson interval would be 0.966 to 0.972 at +15 min (width 0.005) against 0.955 to 0.982 from the block bootstrap (width 0.027). All widths in this paragraph are computed from the unrounded endpoints.

This is not the rule behind the live dashboard's "HUMAN REVIEW REQUIRED" flag (`human_review_required`). That flag comes from the live backend rule (`backend/app/services/prediction.py`): effective confidence = model confidence x clip(0.4 + 0.6 x sensor reliability, 0, 1), times 0.7 when the input looks unusual (out of distribution), flagged below 0.6; when reliability is below 0.25 no forecast is shown at all and the entry is also marked for human review. It flags more. **The flag counts and shares below are lower bounds**: the feature-range half of the out-of-distribution check cannot be recomputed from the stored predictions, so some forecasts the live rule would flag are counted as unflagged here. The accuracy column is not a bound in either direction.

| horizon | no forecast (reliability < 0.25) | low-confidence flag | accuracy of low-confidence flagged (Wilson 95%; zone-day block bootstrap 95%) | needing review, at least |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| +5 min | 29 | 394 | 0.660 (0.612 to 0.705; 0.628 to 0.708) | 423 (2.4%) |
| +10 min | 44 | 394 | 0.660 (0.612 to 0.705; 0.613 to 0.722) | 438 (2.5%) |
| +15 min | 49 | 354 | 0.653 (0.602 to 0.700; 0.593 to 0.734) | 403 (2.3%) |

Meaning: smoothing the last few minutes is a better naive forecast than the last minute alone (best at +15 min: 3-min moving-average persistence, 0.808), and the model still clears it by +0.057; at +5 min the edge over the best smoothing baseline is only +0.015 (+0.004 to +0.025). The longer the horizon, the more the model adds over smoothing.

## 6. Mall counting: calibration vs a detector-free guess

Held-out frames 801-2000 (mean 32.2 annotated people). Intervals: 24 blocks of 50 consecutive held-out frames, 10,000 resamples, seed 0.

| method | uses the detector? | held-out MAE (people per frame, 95% interval) |
|---|---|---|
| YOLO11n raw count | yes | 13.42 (12.58 to 14.26) |
| always say 30.05 (mean annotated count of the same 20 calibration frames) | no | 4.79 (4.11 to 5.55) |
| always say 29.54 (mean of all 800 training frames) | no | 4.94 (4.22 to 5.74) |
| YOLO11n + linear calibration on the 20 frames | yes | 3.33 (3.04 to 3.64) |

Meaning: most of the drop from 13.42 to 3.33 is fixing the detector's systematic under-count; a fixed guess with no detector at all already gets 4.79. The calibrated detector beats that guess by 1.46 people per frame (95% interval 0.95 to 2.01): that margin, not the drop from 13.4, is what the detector adds in this mall, where the held-out count stays between 17 and 53 people. The calibration frames are fixed, so the intervals cover only which held-out frames are drawn.

The app's fallback detector (Apple upper-body), calibrated the same way, reaches 4.45 (4.05 to 4.88), 0.34 better than the fixed guess (95% interval -0.26 to +0.98): not distinguishable from guessing, so the fallback adds little beyond a per-site average.
